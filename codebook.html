Data Codebook

Version: October 14, 2026. Two balanced district-year panels; each contains 160 districts, seven study years, 1,120 rows, and the same nine columns. District and year jointly identify a row. These are fully simulated data, not student records.

Variables

Variable Meaning
district_id Integer district identifier, 1-160. Repeated across seven years.
year Study year, 1-7; not an actual calendar year.
cohort treated_year5, treated_year6, treated_year7, or never, as applicable.
treatment_start_year First treated year. 0 means never treated.
treated_ever 1 for an eventually treated district; otherwise 0.
treated 1 starting in the adoption year and in every subsequent year; otherwise 0.
event_time year - treatment_start_year for eventually treated districts. Negative before adoption, zero at adoption. Missing for never-treated districts: NA in CSV, system missing . in Stata.
outcome_std Continuous observed achievement on a common pre-treatment reference-SD scale.
outcome_raw The same outcome in synthetic score points: 50 + 10 * outcome_std.

Only event_time has missing values, for never-treated districts. There are no adjustment covariates and no generic outcome column. True effects and untreated potential outcomes are kept separately for instructor validation.

Treatment is assigned at district level and never switches off. All districts are untreated in Years 1-4. The new panels retain that pre-treatment structure, not the exact numerical values of the previous 11-year version.

Outcome Scale and Generation

The generator uses seed 20261014, random district effects, a common year trend, and district errors with AR(1) correlation 0.6. Cohort assignment is randomized independently of these untreated outcomes. No district-specific slopes or covariates enter the outcome model. This creates parallel untreated trends in expectation; sample pre-trends need not coincide exactly. Treatment has no anticipation or spillovers.

The untreated outcome’s pooled Years 1-4 mean and sample SD define a single reference scale shared by both simulations. Treatment effects are added afterward. Thus the observed outcome_std need not have mean zero or SD one after treatment. A difference of 0.20 reference SD is exactly 2 raw score points.

The raw scale is an affine transformation, not an observed test instrument. Values are continuous and unbounded; 50 is the pre-treatment reference mean and 10 its SD.

Simulation 1 adds 0.20 reference SD (2 points) in every treated period. Simulation 2 adds 0.10 + 0.10 * event_time + 0.05 * (treatment_start_year - 5) reference SD in treated periods only. These chosen effects illustrate heterogeneous treatment effects; they are not estimates from a real education study.

The true Simple ATT is 2 raw points. Sample estimates will differ because of sampling noise. Earlier cohorts receive more total weight because more of their post-treatment periods are observed. The late cohort has no observed effect beyond event time zero; longer-run dynamic estimates would involve fewer cohorts.
